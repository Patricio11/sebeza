## WHO WE'RE TALKING TO
Three audiences, but **early on, talk mostly to seekers and the general SA public**. They're the story,
the emotion, and the followers. Employers and government come later, warmed by the same feed.

- **Job-seekers** (all ages, all skill levels, low-income to professional, mostly on cheap Androids).
- **The broader SA public** who care about unemployment. They share, they amplify, they become advocates.
- **Employers / recruiters** (watching quietly; posts occasionally speak to them).
- **Government / partners** (watching quietly; the mission + data-intelligence posts speak to them).

Primary language **English**, page region **South Africa**. Sprinkle isiZulu/isiXhosa/Afrikaans phrases
where natural (especially "Sebenza" = "to work"). It signals *this is for South Africans, by someone who
gets it.* Never force it.

## VOICE
- **Warm, honest, human, hopeful. Never corporate, never hype.** You're a solo builder who cares, not a
  faceless startup. That authenticity is your entire edge over a polished competitor.
- **Plain language.** If a matric-leaver on a R1500 phone wouldn't understand it, rewrite it.
- **Proud South African, without kitsch.** Green/gold Mzansi identity, real local references.
- **Show the person behind it.** People follow founders and missions, not logos. Behind-the-scenes is gold.
- **Truthful.** Never claim users/traction you don't have. "We're building," not "thousands trust us."
  (Your Verification-Honesty rule applies to marketing too. It's who you are.)
- **No em-dashes, anywhere.** House rule: they read as AI-written. Full stops, commas, colons, or the
  "·" separator. This applies to captions, graphics, comments, and replies.

## WHAT WE LEARNED FROM ODOO (format, not content)
Odoo's page is mature-product marketing: webinars, feature deep-dives, consistent hashtags, steady
cadence, visual-first posts. **Take the format discipline, drop the content model.** They educate
existing users; you're building a story from zero. Specifically borrow:
- **Consistent cadence** (steady, predictable posting beats sporadic bursts).
- **Every post visual** (image/graphic/short video: Facebook buries text-only posts).
- **A consistent hashtag set** (below) so the brand is searchable and coherent.
- **One clear idea per post** (they never cram; neither should you).
Ignore their webinar/feature-sell model entirely. Wrong stage for you.

## CONTENT PILLARS (the mix)
Rotate these so the feed never gets repetitive. Rough target mix per week:

1. **Mission & Why** (~30%): the unemployment problem, why Sebenza exists, dignity, being seen.
2. **Behind-the-scenes / Founder story** (~25%): building it solo, the journey, honest and human.
   *This is your highest-engagement pillar. People connect to the person.*
3. **What it does / Vision** (~20%): features as *benefits* ("get found for what you can do,"
   "it tells you what to learn next"), the future you're building toward.
4. **Education & value** (~15%): genuinely useful content (CV tips, interview prep, the SA job market)
   that helps people *whether or not* they ever use Sebenza. Builds trust + shareability.
5. **South Africa & community** (~10%): local pride, youth, the economy, real SA context.

## CADENCE (realistic for a solo founder)
- **Start: 3 posts/week.** Sustainable beats ambitious-then-silent. A dead page hurts more than a slow one.
- Suggested rhythm: **Mon** (mission/value), **Wed** (behind-the-scenes/founder), **Fri** (vision/what-it-does).
- **Engage daily-ish** for 10 min: reply to every comment, thank shares, answer questions. Early on,
  *conversation* grows a page faster than posting. Follow + engage with SA unemployment/youth/careers pages.
- Post when your audience is on: generally **early morning (6-8am, commute)** and **evening (7-9pm)** in SA.
